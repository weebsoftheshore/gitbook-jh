# The Judge Call

The basic "Judge Call" is the most basic tool and procedure that judge use in organized play for a variety of competitively-played card games, including Grand Archive. While players are typically knowledgeable enough to play the game, mistakes are inevitable across a hundred actions in each of potentially thousands of matches played during a tournament. Rather than depend on players to resolve errors, miscommunications, and potentially poor behavior which might harm the integrity of the tournament, Judges attend these points of conflict and act with [impartiality](impartiality.md) to correct a game (as much as is possible) and apply penalties to keep a tournament fair.



#### The Call

While Judges can tend to issues that arise without being prompted, the Judge Call arises most commonly from a player's request. Briefly, a judge call that takes place covers several steps in a regular procedure:

1. A player will either verbally or physically signal for a judge, requesting their presence to a match or situation.
2. The player who initiated the call will briefly explain and give context to the situation and reason for the call.
3. As an attending judge, it is prudent to ask clarifying questions and gather more information about the situation. Please use your best judgement to decide whether it is necessary to interview players separately and/or in private when asking these questions.
4. The attending judge issues a final outcome for the call, including performing any corrective procedures, and announcing the infraction for the violation and its penalty (if applicable)
5. Lastly, the player has a right to **appeal** the ruling, regardless of how confident and correct the attending judge is. The only exception to this is if the attending judge is also the Head Judge of a tournament.



Breaking down each of the steps further, let's start with the initiation of the call.

1. &#x20;Both players and judges should hold decorum in the tournament hall or gaming venue when initiating and responding to a call, respectively. Player expectations are covered within the TRG, so we'll reserve this section to talk about responsibilities of Judges for all parts discussed. Judges should make their way towards the call calmly and intentionally. Signal to the player that you've acknowledged their request for the call, either by waving or by speaking in the player's direction. Be careful to not yell over crowds and nearby players.
2. For the second part, listen to the players carefully while they explain the reason for the call. This part is particularly important as there is significant nuance in the words players will use to express themselves and explaining the situation. Additionally, some players may not be as proficient in the language(s) you speak, so try to understand the core principles behind the call. This may require actively filtering out some information that _might_ be irrelevant, unhelpful, or actively damaging to the judge call. Consider a few of the following:

* What kind of question is the player asking?
  * Is it a clarification question?
  * Is it a hypothetical question?&#x20;
  * Is it a situation where you will need their opponent's (or other nearby players'/spectators' testimonies?
* Is the player trying to hint at a specific violation?
  * If so, what kind of violation?
  * Are they trying to persuade you to punish the opponent in any way?
* Does the player need assistance conveying their thoughts? Consider using a language translation app, if needed.

3. Taking extra steps to be attentive in the second part will help you investigate properly in the 3rd part of the call. It is important to ask the right clarification questions and get testimonies from both players. If you suspect the player is trying to ask a hypothetical question, try rephrasing their question where you can reduce it to a clarification or interaction-based question. If the call involves private information, take players aside to discuss privately to avoid leaking information that otherwise would not have been known to the opponent. Importantly, **talk to both players!** Many calls involve information that heavily depends on getting the right context and information. Ignoring crucial testimony means you have no way of cross-referencing the information you were given from the initial player. While it is understandable to expect that the other players will correct the initial player on any wrong information, **do not assume this is the case**!
4. After you've investigated enough and are ready to deliver your ruling, make sure you first deliver your thoughts, justifications, intentions for corrective procedures, and any applicable infractions that are being penalized **before you give them**. Corrective procedures, in particular, sometimes can't be undone. If the call is appealed and the corrective procedure prescribed by the Head Judge differs, the game can be irreversible or requires more work to rewind the procedure to do another. Deliver your reasoning calmly and with neutral language. Be confident! At the same time, understand something very important: judges are not expected to be perfect and have a perfect understanding of every single rule and interactions. If you feel a call is beyond your abilities as a judge to resolve, do not be afraid to escalate it or, at the very least, discuss it with your more senior judges.
5. If the player still wishes to appeal, that is their right! If a player does not appear to be swayed or understanding of your ruling, it may not be a bad idea to prompt them that they are able to appeal. Allowing them to have the opportunity in moments like these will help players feel better and more comfortable with whatever ruling they are given and often are appreciative of the offer. When a player does appeal, try to summarize the situation for your Head Judge, but try to leave out any personal opinions about the call unless prompted. This lets the Head Judge be more objective and less biased when approaching the call and investigating it on their own.
   1. If the Head Judge is the attending judge, the player can't appeal!
